When a company website is the right choice.

It makes sense when

  • The company offers several services, serves different audiences or has more than one location.
  • You sell to other businesses and need to convey credibility before the meeting.
  • You want to be found on Google by service, city or specialty.
  • Your current site is outdated and no longer reflects the size of the company.

Another format works better when

  • You have one main offer and a campaign focused on contact or purchase: see Landing Page.
  • The goal is to sell products directly on the site: see E-commerce.
  • You need a logged-in area, online proposals or internal management: see Systems & SaaS.

Company website FAQ.

How much does a company website cost?

The average investment is $420, with projects between $180 and $990. A one-page site ranges from $180 to $290; multi-page, the most common range, from $320 to $540; and the strategic presence, with content architecture and copy, starts at $900.

How is it different from a landing page?

A landing page has one offer and one goal. A company website presents the whole company, with dedicated pages for each topic, and serves different audiences: clients, partners, suppliers and job candidates.

Will I be able to update the site myself?

Yes, when the project includes an editable dashboard: text, images, services and portfolio are accessible without touching code. Without a dashboard, changes are covered by the six-month maintenance plan.

Will the site show up on Google?

The site is delivered with technical SEO: titles, metadata, sitemap and Search Console set up, plus attention to loading speed. Ranking also depends on content, competition and time. The work builds a foundation to be found, with no ranking guarantee.

Are copy and photos included?

Copy for every page is included in Strategic presence. In the other tiers, the content you send is reviewed and organized. Photos come from the company's own library or licensed stock; photo shoots are not included.

Does your website reflect the value of your company?

Tell me what your company offers, who it needs to reach and how the current site serves that goal. We align the page structure and scope before defining the quote.
